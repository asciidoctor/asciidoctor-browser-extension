= Capacity Planning

Ferry's throughput is governed by ordinary queueing theory, so you can size a cluster with a
calculator instead of a load test.

== Utilization

A worker with service rate stem:[\mu] jobs/second handling an arrival rate stem:[\lambda]
jobs/second has utilization stem:[\rho = \lambda / \mu]. Keep stem:[\rho] under about 0.7 in
production; above that, queueing delay grows much faster than throughput does.

== Little's Law

[stem]
++++
L = \lambda W
++++

The average number of jobs in the system (stem:[L]), the arrival rate (stem:[\lambda]), and the
average time a job spends in the system (stem:[W]) are related by Little's Law. This is why
<<_configuration_reference,`FERRY_MAX_CONCURRENCY`>> alone doesn't fix latency: if stem:[\lambda]
keeps growing, stem:[W] grows too unless you also add workers.

== Long-Run Behavior

As the observation window grows, the total number of jobs processed approaches:

[stem]
++++
\lim_{N \to \infty} \sum_{i=0}^{N} \text{jobs}_i
++++

A recurrence like this one shows up surprisingly often when modeling exponential-backoff jitter
across retries:

[latexmath]
++++
k_{n+1} = n^2 + k_n^2 - k_{n-1}
++++

Keep the arrival rate λ~in~ comfortably below the service rate μ~out~ — the gap between them is
your safety margin during traffic spikes.
